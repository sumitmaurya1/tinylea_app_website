You search for a free learning app, install one, and your four-year-old plays happily for a week. Then a padlock appears on level five, and a cheerful voice explains that the rest costs money every month.

It is not a scam, exactly. The app was free to download. But "free to download" and "free to use" are different claims, and the store listing does not shout about which one you are getting.

Here is how to tell the difference in about a minute.

## The short answer

Kids apps make money in four ways: ads, a paywall partway through, a subscription (usually after a free trial), or a one-off price. Two labels on the Play Store listing — **Contains ads** and **In-app purchases** — tell you which combination applies. Check those before installing, not after.

## The four models

**Free, ad-supported.** Everything is unlocked; ads pay for it. The cost is attention and the quality of the ads, which varies enormously.

**Free up to a point, then a paywall.** The opening levels or topics are free, the rest is not. This is the most common one, and the one that causes the padlock-on-level-five moment.

**Free trial, then a subscription.** Often a few days free, then a monthly or yearly charge, and the trial usually auto-renews unless you cancel. This is the one that produces unexpected charges.

**Paid upfront.** You pay once and that is that. Rare in this category now, and often the best value if you find one.

None of these is dishonest in itself. Developers have to eat. The problem is only when the model is hidden until your child is already attached to the app.

## The two labels that tell you everything

On a Google Play listing, under the install button, you will find some combination of:

- **Contains ads**
- **In-app purchases**

Read them like this:

| Labels | What you are getting |
|---|---|
| Contains ads, no in-app purchases | Ad-supported, fully free to use |
| In-app purchases, no ads | Paywall or subscription, no ads |
| Both | Ads now, and something to buy as well |
| Neither | Genuinely free, or paid upfront |

If a listing says **In-app purchases**, tap through to the price range Google shows. A range topping out at a few hundred rupees usually means a one-off unlock. A recurring monthly figure means a subscription.

<Callout title="Check the screenshots too">
Store screenshots are chosen by the developer, so they show the best part of the app. Scroll to the end. If the last screenshot is a pricing screen or a "Premium" badge, you have your answer before you install anything.
</Callout>

## The trial trap

The pattern worth knowing: a short free trial, a card added at sign-up, and automatic renewal when the trial ends.

Plenty of parents sign up intending to cancel, and then the week gets away from them. If you do start a trial, set a reminder for a day before it ends, and check Google Play's subscriptions page rather than trusting the app to remind you — most do not.

## Ad-supported is a trade, not a free lunch

If you choose a free, ad-supported app, you are paying with your child's attention instead of money. That is a legitimate trade, but it is worth checking what you are getting:

- Are ads **clearly marked**, and easy to close?
- Do they appear between activities, or interrupt them?
- Do they lead to a store page, a browser or a game with a buy button?
- Is the app signed up to Google's **Families** ad policy, which restricts what can be shown to children?

An app that shows a full-screen video ad after every level is charging you more than one with a small banner, whatever the price tag says. The [seven-point checklist](/blog/how-to-choose-a-learning-app-for-kids) covers how to test ads properly, alongside the other things worth checking.

## Five questions before you install

1. Does the listing say **In-app purchases**? If so, what is the price range?
2. Is there a **subscription**, and does it start with a trial?
3. If it is ad-supported, how **often** do ads appear, and what kind?
4. Can my child **reach a payment screen** on their own, or is there a parent gate?
5. Does it work **offline**, or does it need a connection to show me anything?

## If you have already been charged

Subscriptions bought through the Play Store are managed in one place — your **Google Play account's subscriptions page**, not inside the app. Cancelling there stops future charges.

For a charge you did not intend, Play has a refund request process in your order history. It is worth trying, especially if a child made the purchase. Then turn on purchase authentication in the Play Store settings, so a password is needed for every purchase on that device. That one setting prevents most accidental spending.
