You want to help your child learn at home, but planning another lesson after a busy day can feel like too much. Start with something already in the room: a favourite toy, a picture book or a pile of socks.

These seven learning activities pair letters, numbers, colours and shapes with everyday play. Choose one that interests your child, repeat a favourite, and leave the rest for another day. There is no worksheet to finish or weekly target to meet.

If you would like an app to use alongside these activities, **TinyLearn is a free, ad-supported Android learning app designed for ages 2–8**. It includes English, Hindi and Marathi alphabets, numbers 1–100 in all three languages, and topics such as colours, shapes and phonics. Its lessons and games work offline.

## 1. Find a letter from your child's name

Write your child's name in large letters on paper. Pick the first letter and look for it together on a book cover or food packet. Let your child point, circle it on your paper, or draw their own version.

**Add TinyLearn:** open the English alphabet topic and tap the matching letter to hear it. Then put the phone down and return to your letter hunt.

If your child already recognises the letter, invite them to find another. For more variations, see our [guide to teaching the alphabet](/blog/how-to-teach-alphabet-to-toddlers).

## 2. Count toys into a basket

Place a few large toys on the floor. Move them into a basket one at a time, saying a number for each toy. Ask, “How many did we put away?” Count again together if your child wants to check.

**Add TinyLearn:** listen to the same numbers in English, Hindi or Marathi, then count the toys again using your chosen language. Start with a handful of objects; there is no need to reach 100 because the app includes it.

For a child who wants a different challenge, take one toy out and ask how many remain.

## 3. Go on a colour hunt

Choose one colour and take turns finding it around the room. A blue cushion, a blue book and a blue shirt all count. Your child can point to objects without collecting them.

**Add TinyLearn:** explore the colours topic together before choosing the colour for your hunt.

Let your child choose the next colour or become the person who checks your finds. Our [colour activities guide](/blog/how-to-teach-colours-to-toddlers) has more ways to continue the game.

## 4. Make a picture from shapes

Draw a circle, a square and a triangle on paper. Turn them into something together: a square house with a triangle roof, or a face inside a circle. Ask your child what they would like to add.

**Add TinyLearn:** look at the shapes topic or try Match the Shapes together. Afterwards, look for those shapes in your drawing.

Keep the conversation open: “What could this circle become?” gives your child room to invent their own picture.

## 5. Explore a Hindi or Marathi letter

Choose a letter from a family member's name or a word your family uses. Write it down and say a familiar word that uses it. If you have a book in that language, see whether you can find the letter together.

**Add TinyLearn:** open Hindi Varnamala or Marathi Varnamala and tap a letter to hear it. Listen together and invite your child to join in.

You can stay with one letter for several sessions. Visit our [language overview](/languages) to see what is included, or use the [Hindi alphabet guide](/blog/learn-hindi-for-kids-varnamala-guide) for more ideas.

## 6. Play an animal guessing game

Take turns making an animal sound or acting out an animal. The other person guesses. Add a question such as “Does it have wings?” or “How does it move?” when your child wants more clues.

**Add TinyLearn:** explore the animals topic and its sounds, then choose an animal to act out away from the screen.

Your child can also draw the animal or find it in a picture book. The activity can be as simple as naming one favourite animal together.

## 7. Let your child lead a repeat session

Offer two activities from the week: “Shall we count toys or make a shape picture?” Let your child choose and show you how to start.

**Add TinyLearn:** revisit the matching topic or a game you have already explored together. See our [mini-games overview](/games) if you want to preview the options.

Notice something specific you did together: “We found three circles in your picture.” There is no need to turn the session into a quiz.
