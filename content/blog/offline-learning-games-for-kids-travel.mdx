## What to prepare before you travel

Install and open the app at home. Explore a lesson together so your child knows where to start, rather than meeting a new interface in a busy departure lounge.

Use this quick packing checklist:

1. **Choose a familiar topic.** Try shapes, a few letters or numbers your child enjoys.
2. **Test offline play.** Turn on aeroplane mode and open the lesson and game you plan to use.
3. **Check the sound.** Tap-to-hear lessons use audio, so decide how you will listen in a shared space.
4. **Agree on a stopping point.** “One game, then our picture book” gives the activity a clear ending.
5. **Pack a screen-free follow-up.** A notebook and crayons can turn a shape or letter into something to draw together.

For an overview of the app before installing, visit the [TinyLearn download page](/download).

## Five learning activities for the journey

Pick whichever activity matches your child's interest and current skills. There is no need to finish every topic or make the whole journey a lesson.

### 1. Find a shape, then spot it around you

Start with TinyLearn's shapes topic or **Match the Shapes** game. Afterwards, look for a circle on a sign, a rectangle on a suitcase or a square in a picture book.

Ask, “Can you find another one?” You can also draw a shape and let your child turn it into a face, house or vehicle. Our [guide to teaching shapes](/blog/how-to-teach-shapes-to-toddlers) has more ideas for home practice.

### 2. Count a small collection

Explore the numbers topic, then count something nearby: fingers, bags or characters in a book. Start with a small collection your child can comfortably count, even though the app includes numbers 1–100.

Try asking, “How many altogether?” after counting. For more activities, see [teaching kids to count](/blog/teaching-kids-to-count-1-to-100).

### 3. Choose a letter from your child's name

Open the English alphabet topic and find the first letter of your child's name. Listen together, then look for the same letter on a ticket, sign or book cover.

Keep the hunt to one or two letters. If your child wants to explore sounds next, use the phonics topic and our [phonics activities for home](/blog/how-to-teach-phonics-at-home).

### 4. Bring a home language along

For families using Hindi or Marathi, a trip can be a chance to revisit a familiar letter or practise counting together. TinyLearn includes Hindi Varnamala, Marathi Varnamala and numbers 1–100 in English, Hindi and Marathi.

Choose a number in the app, listen, and use it while counting objects together. If you are visiting relatives, invite them to join the same activity later. The app provides practice; conversations with family give that practice a personal connection.

Explore the [language topics](/languages) or our [guide for families teaching Hindi and Marathi abroad](/blog/teaching-hindi-marathi-abroad-nri-families).

### 5. Take turns with a word game

For a child who is ready for word activities, explore **Pop the Word** or **Sentence Rocket** together. Ask them to explain a choice, or take turns choosing an answer.

If it feels too difficult, switch to a familiar lesson. You can preview the [TinyLearn mini-games](/games) before choosing one for your journey.

## Make room for activities without a phone

Once you put the device away, continue with an “I spy” colour hunt, a shared story or a drawing. For example, follow a colours lesson by asking your child to find three blue things in a book.

You do not need a phone for every waiting period. Save our [learning games to play anywhere without toys](/blog/learning-games-to-play-anywhere-no-toys) for more ideas before you travel.
